# The q = 28 restriction cannot extend to the archived residual

## Result and scope

For the archived binary length-37 residual B, in the branch where the left projection A has one-dimensional message kernel, **q = 28 is impossible**. Together with the already established q ≥ 16 and q ≡ 0 (mod 4), this leaves q ∈ {16,20,24} for this branch.

This is an exact exclusion of one remaining parameter value, not a new code, an exclusion of the whole branch, or a resolution of binary [64,11,27] existence. No conclusion is obtained for the other three q values, the kernel-zero branch, or other residuals. No novelty or external peer-review claim is made.

The November 8 formal survivor with integer coordinates (6,14,4,7,13,0,18), equivalently counts (32,28,72,32,18,44,18), still passes the 11 count bounds, all 1,064 nonnegative integral split coefficients, and the November 8 column cut with slack 3. The new result excludes every actual lift realizing q = 28, including every labelling of that formal point. It does not retract the point's formal feasibility.

The argument was independently derived after reading the November 8 plan. The archived November 8 Claude exchange established the restricted-code classification, not this new extension obstruction. Any later actual exchange should be attributed separately.

## Why q = 28 gives three zero coordinates and four-message blocks

Identify S with F2^5 by s ↦ s << 4. For the restriction of A to S, let N be the number of nonzero columns, with multiplicities m_v for nonzero five-bit column labels v. If q = 28, the first moment is

16N = 28·12 + 3·16 = 384,

so N = 24. There are exactly three coordinates that vanish on S. They need not vanish on all messages.

Let P = sum_v binom(m_v,2). The second moment of the restriction is

28·12² + 3·16² = 8(N² + N + 2P).

With N = 24 both sides without the 16P term equal 4,800, so P = 0: the 24 nonzero restricted columns are distinct. Their complement among the 31 nonzero vectors consists of seven columns. Its evaluation has weight 4 on 28 nonzero messages and weight 0 on the other three. Including the zero message, the evaluation kernel has size four, hence the seven columns span a three-dimensional space U. They are exactly U \ {0}.

Consequently the active restricted columns are F2^5 \ U. Set K = U-perp, a two-dimensional subspace of S. No specific orientation of U or K is assumed.

Choose the transversal u = t XOR (s << 4) modulo span(h), with t ∈ {0,...,15} and s ∈ {0,...,31}. For a fixed quotient label t, let z(t) be the weight on the three coordinates that vanish on S, and let w_t(s) = wt((t XOR (s << 4))A).

The active-coordinate character sum is

F_t(s) = sum_(v outside U) (−1)^(e_v + s·v),

where e_v is the active-coordinate value at t. Then

w_t(s) = 12 + z(t) − F_t(s)/2.

Orthogonality on F2^5 gives

sum_s (w_t(s) − 12 − z(t))² = 32·24/4 = 192.     (1)

For every coset a+K, each active v lies outside K-perp = U, so its character sums to zero on a+K. Thus

sum_(s in a+K) w_t(s) = 48 + 4z(t).              (2)

These identities hold for every actual extension of the classified restriction. They are not inferred from sampling.

## Each of those quotient labels must have z(t) = 2

Fix t ∈ T, and let H be its forced-14 hyperplane. There are two cases, covering every K.

1. **K lies in the direction of H.** Some K-coset is contained in H. Its four weights are all 14, so (2) gives 56 = 48 + 4z(t), hence z(t) = 2.

2. **K is transverse to H.** Every one of the eight K-cosets contains two forced weights 14 and two free weights x,y ∈ {10,14,18}. Formula (2) becomes 28+x+y = 48+4z(t). As z(t) is a weight on three coordinates, it is in {0,1,2,3}.

   - z = 0 forces x = y = 10. The four centered-square contributions about 12 total 16, hence all eight blocks total 128, contradicting (1).
   - z = 1 forces {x,y} = {10,14}. The contributions about 13 total 12 per block, hence 96 overall, contradicting (1).
   - z = 3 forces {x,y} = {14,18}. The contributions about 15 again total 12 per block, hence 96 overall, contradicting (1).
   - Only z = 2 remains. Its individual block square sums can be 0 or 32; (1) alone requires six blocks of the second kind. No sufficiency for a real extension is asserted.

Thus z(t) = 2 for all ten labels in T. Notice the z = 0 case is a second-moment contradiction, not a lower-bound contradiction: its block sum 48 is attainable.

The generic two-case argument covers all orientations. A redundant exact audit enumerates all 155 two-dimensional K and verifies all 1,550 (K,t) cases: 350 parallel and 1,200 transverse.

## Five binary triples cannot have all pairwise distances two

The three coordinates that vanish on S define a linear map

L : F2^4 → F2^3,

because they also annihilate h and so descend to the quotient. By definition, z(t) = wt(L(t)).

Take C = {1,4,7,13,15}. Its ten distinct pairwise XORs are precisely T. Therefore for any distinct a,b in C,

d_H(L(a),L(b)) = wt(L(a XOR b)) = z(a XOR b) = 2.

This would give five distinct binary triples whose pairwise distances are all even. They must lie in one parity class of F2^3, but each parity class contains only four vectors. Contradiction.

The finite enumeration of 816 unordered three-column maps is only a redundant check of this terminal contradiction. The proof does not infer global nonexistence from a failed finite matrix search.
