# Independent verification: residual lifting

## Scope

This checks an exact reformulation of a possible binary [64,11,27] code and a single published residual candidate. It does not construct such a code, rule out that candidate, classify all residuals, or improve the known 26–27 distance interval. The residual construction and elementary rank arguments are not claimed as new theorems.

## Exact criterion

Choose a weight-27 word c in C. After a coordinate permutation and basis choice, write G=[1^27 0^37; A B]. Puncturing the support of c has kernel exactly its span: any nonzero word supported in those 27 positions must occupy all of them, because the minimum distance is 27. Hence rank(B)=10.

For each nonzero u, let a=wt(uA) and r=wt(uB). The two associated words have weights a+r and 27-a+r. Both have weight at least 27 exactly when 27-r<=a<=r. The remaining nonzero word is c itself. This proves necessity and sufficiency, not merely a heuristic filter. It forces r>=14 and, when r=14, a in {13,14}. The known [37,10] upper bound 14 then makes the residual minimum distance exactly 14.

## Rank restriction

The kernel of projection onto the left 27 coordinates is a length-37 binary subcode with minimum distance at least 27. It has dimension at most one: two independent words x,y would give three nonzero words x,y,x+y with total weight at least 81, whereas their total weight is at most 2*37=74. Thus rank([1^27;A])>=10, implying rank(A)>=9. The projection rank is invariant for the chosen support split; A itself depends on the selected lifts of the residual basis.

If B has no word of weight at least 27, that projection kernel must be zero and rank([1;A])=11, rank(A)=10. For the actual candidate below, there is one such word, so this stronger special case does not apply.

## Candidate and exhaustive small controls

Independent enumeration of the source 12x39 matrix in Grassl's table gave 4096 words and minimum distance 14. Shortening coordinates 38 and 39 gave 1024 words, exactly the span of the published derived basis. Its distribution is:

| Weight | Count |
|---|---:|
|0|1|
|14|160|
|16|142|
|18|320|
|20|160|
|22|160|
|24|80|
|32|1|

The unique word with weight at least 27 is hexadecimal 0xffffffff, with bit 0 corresponding to coordinate 1. For a lift of this particular residual, the left-projection kernel is therefore either zero or generated by (0,h), where h is that word. Neither case has been excluded. Its 160 weight-14 words imply at least 161 weight-27 words (including c) and at least 160 weight-28 words in any valid lift.

`audit_candidate_public.py` independently enumerates the published derived basis and checks this distribution and unique heavy word. It does not independently reconstruct the source matrix unless that source is separately supplied and checked.

`audit_toy.py` enumerates all 64 binary 2x3 matrices A over B=(3,5), an elementary [3,2,2] code. Direct enumeration of all seven nonzero full-code words agrees with the interval criterion in every case: 24 valid [6,3,3] lifts, 40 failures, zero disagreements. This is an exhaustive toy check, not a length-64 search.

## Correction to the generated random-control script

An independent rerun of the unchanged generated `residual_lift_control.py` reproduced 120000 agreements, 479 true cases, 119521 false cases, Hamming-control minimum distance 3, and a one-bit-flip negative-control minimum distance 2. This is a rerun of the script, not a claim that an original run log was downloaded.

The output label “criterion <=> d(C)>=3” needs qualification for randomly generated dependent rows. Its `direct_min` skips the zero coefficient vector but not zero codewords arising from dependencies. For example, with w=3 and A=B=[0,0,0], the span has minimum nonzero weight 3, but `direct_min` returns 0. The tested predicate is actually full generator rank 4 AND minimum distance at least 3. The Hamming and fixed-full-rank-B controls are unaffected. The original script is preserved; this paragraph corrects its interpretation.
